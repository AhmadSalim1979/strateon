## SECTION B: QIYADON PRODUCT/POSITIONING AUDIT

### What Qiyadon Gets Right

**1. Positioning is sharp.** "We do not give you another tool. We operate the follow-up layer of your pipeline." This is the right message for the ICP. Founders are tired of tools. They want accountability. Qiyadon named this correctly.

**2. The pain statement is real.** "Most inbound leads go dark within 72 hours — not because the founder doesn't care, because closing is a full-time job and following up is a second one." This lands. Founders recognize themselves in it.

**3. The tier structure is sensible.** Starter ($300), Growth ($750), Scale ($1,500+) gives SMBs a logical upgrade path. The feature gaps between tiers are real and defensible.

**4. The "7 days to first report" promise sets clear expectations.** Founders know when they'll see value. No ambiguity about timelines.

**5. No credit card for trial is the right call.** Reduces friction. They just need to lower the lead minimum.

### What Qiyadon Gets Wrong — Critical

**1. No clients. Zero.** Every claim on the website is aspirational. "Every Friday, real numbers" — no real Friday report exists. "If a lead goes dark, that is our failure" — no proof that failure has ever been addressed. The gap between marketing and reality is a credibility crisis.

**2. No payment system.** No Stripe. No payment link. No Buy button. The website cannot process a transaction. If a prospect says yes today, Qiyadon cannot take their money today.

**3. No human face.** The website footer says "Ahmad Salim — Founding Board Member." That's it. No photo, no bio, no LinkedIn. The C-suite is AI agents. B2B trust is built on LinkedIn. Qiyadon has none.

**4. Trial requires 20 leads.** The "no commitment" trial actually requires committing 20 live leads before the trial starts. This filters out the curious — the best prospects.

**5. 7-day onboarding is slow.** The "same day" value proposition is stronger than the "7 days" promise. A founder who wants to test something today doesn't want to wait a week.

**6. No real-time pipeline visibility below Scale tier.** Growth ($750/month) gets daily brief via WhatsApp, but no portal. A VP Sales who wants to check pipeline status mid-week has no tool. Qiyadon reserves this for $1,500+/month.

**7. No outbound engine.** The website exists. No one is driving traffic to it. Qiyadon is 100% dependent on inbound — which is effectively zero without SEO, content, or social presence.
