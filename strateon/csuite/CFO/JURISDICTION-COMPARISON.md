## Executive Summary

For Qiyadon's specific situation — bootstrapped, global services (not trading), Pipeline Execution-as-a-Service, EU/UK/MENA client focus — **Dubai/UAE in a free zone is the clear winner** on total cost and operational flexibility. England & Wales is the backup if EU credibility is non-negotiable.

## 1. Delaware (USA)

### Annual Costs
| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Registered agent | ~$100–200/yr |
| Annual franchise tax + reporting | ~$300–800/yr (varies by shares/Revenue) |
| Accounting / tax filing (US + foreign) | ~$1,500–3,000/yr |
| **Total** | **~$1,900–4,000/yr** |

### Corporate Tax
- **Federal:** 21% flat on worldwide income
- **Delaware:** No state corporate income tax for companies operating outside Delaware
- **Reality:** If you're managed/operated from outside the US, some states (especially California, New York) can assert "doing business" nexus and tax you. With EU/UK/MENA clients and no US office, you *may* escape US tax — but the IRS can still assert worldwide taxation on US citizens/residents. **You need a US tax strategist to confirm your exposure.**

### Personal Income Tax on Founder (Ahmad Salim)
- **If US person:** Top marginal rate 37% federal + state
- **If non-US person with no US-source income:** $0 US federal income tax
- **Key risk:** If you're a US person or green card holder, the US taxes you on worldwide income regardless of where you live. This is a massive hidden cost.

### Bank Account
- **Moderately easy** — US LLCs can open US bank accounts, but most EU/UK banks are wary of US-incorporated entities due to FATCA compliance overhead. Expect higher friction with European banks.

### Credibility
- **EU/UK clients:** Moderate. Some EU corporates dislike US entities due to CLOUD Act concerns (US government can theoretically access data held by US companies). Not a dealbreaker.
- **MENA clients:** Moderate. Dubai/London are perceived as more natural for MENA work.

### VAT/GST
- **US:** No federal VAT. Some states have "use tax" but not relevant for services exports. **No VAT compliance burden.**
- **Downside:** US companies rarely charge VAT, which can be a credibility signal for EU B2B clients who expect VAT numbers.

### Key Risks for Qiyadon
- If Ahmad is a US person: personal worldwide tax exposure is severe
- US companies face FATCA/FBAR reporting if you open foreign bank accounts
- Delaware is fine for US-focused businesses; less natural for EU/MENA-focused services

## 2. England & Wales (UK)

### Annual Costs
| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Companies House filing fee | ~£13/yr (small company) |
| Registered office address | ~£100–300/yr |
| Accountancy (statutory accounts + CT600) | ~£600–1,500/yr |
| **Total** | **~£700–1,800/yr (~€820–2,100)** |

### Corporate Tax
- **25%** on all profits (small profits rate abolished above £250K; companies under £50K still get 19%, but with global income this gets complex)
- **Reality:** For a bootstrapped services business doing ~$100–500K revenue, expect 19–25% on profits. No territorial exemption for foreign income until you leave the UK entirely (Closing Year rules).

### Personal Income Tax on Founder
- **Tax-free allowance:** ~£12,570/yr
- **Basic rate:** 20% up to ~£50,270
- **Higher rate:** 40% up to ~£125,140
- **Additional rate:** 45% above £125,140
- **National Insurance:** 8% on earnings over ~£12,570 ( Class 4 for self-employed in a company)
- **Dividends:** 8.75% basic / 33.75% higher / 39.35% additional
- For a bootstrapped founder paying themselves a small salary + dividends: relatively efficient if income is low.

### Bank Account
- **Very easy.** UK is one of the world's best banking jurisdictions. Wise, Revolut, Barclays, NatWest — all straightforward for UK LTD. EU banks also comfortable with UK LTDs.

### Credibility
- **EU/UK clients:** **Very high.** UK LTD is the gold standard for EU/UK B2B. Clients trust it immediately.
- **MENA clients:** Good, though less "regional" than UAE.

### VAT/GST
- **VAT registration mandatory** once turnover exceeds £90,000/yr (or voluntary before).
- For global services: VAT on B2B services has complex place-of-supply rules (B2B is usually zero-rated if customer is VAT-registered in EU). **Compliance overhead exists.**
- **If turnover <£90K:** no VAT obligation — low burden.

### Key Risks
- 25% corporation tax is expensive relative to UAE/Delaware (but 19% if you're under £50K profits)
- UK is post-Brexit: no longer has EU VAT "simplification" — EU B2B invoicing is more complex
- UK is tightening rules on "non-resident landlords" and UK company structures

## 3. Dubai / UAE (Free Zone)

### Annual Costs
| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Free zone company license | ~$3,000–8,000/yr (varies by free zone and activity) |
| Free zone office/virtual office | ~$1,000–3,000/yr |
| Registered agent / sponsor (if needed) | ~$2,000–5,000/yr (for local sponsor arrangements) |
| Accounting + audit | ~$1,500–3,000/yr |
| **Total** | **~$5,500–16,000/yr** |

> **Note:** Dubai free zones have gotten more competitive. DMCC, IFZA, DAFZA offer packages in the $3,000–7,000/yr range for service companies. Setup is one-time $2,000–5,000. Ongoing is mainly license + virtual office.

### Corporate Tax
- **0%** corporate tax in free zones (UAE has 0% CT for profits under AED 375,000 (~€94,000); above that, 9% for qualifying income — but free zones generally remain 0% for most free zone entities)
- **New UAE CT law (2023):** 9% on profits above AED 375K for mainland and certain free zones — but qualifying "qualifying income" from free zone activities often stays exempt.
- **Foreign-sourced income:** UAE follows a territorial system — foreign income of UAE entities is not taxed.
- **Reality for Qiyadon:** Most free zone entities pay **$0 corporate tax** for the foreseeable future.

### Personal Income Tax on Founder
- **0%** personal income tax. Ever. No capital gains tax. No wealth tax.
- This is the single biggest personal tax saving vs. any EU/UK/US jurisdiction.

### Bank Account
- **Moderately easy, improving.** UAE banks (Emirates NBD, ADCB, Mashreq) are increasingly open to free zone companies. **Challenge:** UAE banks require in-person account opening for most entities, or a UAE-based relationship. 
- **Workaround:** Multi-currency business accounts via **Wise Business, Payoneer, or Stripe** — these are accessible globally without UAE residency and are practical for a services business.
- **Alternatively:** Open a UK Wise account (easy, even as a UAE entity owner) — gives you UK sort code/account + local EUR/USD transfers. Many UAE free zone companies use Wise + a UAE bank account.

### Credibility
- **EU/UK clients:** **Growing.** Dubai companies are increasingly respected. Some conservative EU corporates still prefer UK or NL entities, but for global services this is diminishing.
- **MENA clients:** **Very high.** Dubai is the regional hub. MENA clients immediately trust a Dubai entity.

### VAT/GST
- **VAT registration mandatory** once turnover exceeds AED 375,000 (~€94,000).
- **5% UAE VAT** on B2C services. For B2B services, reverse charge applies (customer accounts for VAT).
- For bootstrapped Qiyadon (<€100K revenue): likely no VAT registration required for years.
- **No European VAT complexity** — if you don't have EU clients requiring VAT numbers, you avoid the entire EU VAT compliance apparatus.

### Key Risks
- You need to physically be in UAE (or have a local sponsor) to manage operations — not ideal if you're EU-based
- UAE banks: account opening takes time (weeks), requires documents
- For non-GCC clients, some EU corporates will want a "proper" European entity — this is a real (but manageable) friction point
- UAE is not yet on OECD "whitelist" for some compliance purposes, but is FATF compliant

## 4. Germany

### Annual Costs
| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Commercial register (Handelsregister) | ~€50–150/yr |
| Notary fees (for GmbH formation) | ~€500–1,500 one-time |
| Annual accounting (GmbH requires double-entry) | ~€2,000–5,000/yr |
| Tax advisory | ~€1,000–2,500/yr |
| **Total** | **~€3,000–7,500/yr** |

### Corporate Tax
- **~30% effective rate** (15% corporate tax + 15% trade tax/Gewerbesteuer, varying by municipality)
- **Solidarity surcharge:** 5.5% on top of corporate tax (so ~15.75% base + trade tax)
- **Realistic effective rate:** 29–33% depending on municipality (Berlin ~30%, Munich ~30–31%)
- **Foreign income:** Germany taxes worldwide income. Territorial system is NOT available without leaving Germany permanently.

### Personal Income Tax on Founder
- **0–45%** marginal (basic rate 14%, then rises through 24%, 32%, 38%, 42%, 45%)
- Above ~€277,826: 45% top marginal rate
- **Trade tax:** deductible as business expense, but still significant personal burden
- For a bootstrapped founder with low income: the first ~€11,604/yr is tax-free (Grundfreibetrag), then 14–24% until ~€66,760.

### Bank Account
- **Very easy.** German banks (Deutsche, Commerzbank, Sparkasse) open accounts for German GmbHs readily. EU payments are seamless.

### Credibility
- **EU clients:** **Highest possible.** German GmbH = maximum EU credibility. Some EU contracts literally require a German or EU entity.
- **MENA clients:** Moderate. Good but less "regional." Dubai has more cachet in MENA.

### VAT/GST
- **19% German VAT (MwSt).** B2C services in Germany require VAT. B2B reverse charge applies within EU.
- For global services exported to non-EU clients: export is zero-rated. EU VAT compliance is complex but well-documented.
- **If all clients are non-EU:** minimal German VAT burden.

### Key Risks
- **30% corporate tax is the most expensive** on this list
- Double-entry accounting mandatory for GmbH — expensive to maintain
- German entity requires a local managing director (Geschäftsführer) — either resident or you hire someone
- Complex labor law, social security contributions if you hire in Germany
- Stricter data protection (BDSG on top of GDPR) — operational overhead

## Recommendation

### 🥇 Primary: **Dubai / UAE (Free Zone)**

**Reasoning for Qiyadon's specific situation:**

1. **$0 personal income tax** — this is the single biggest financial win. As a bootstrapped founder, you'll be taking profit out of the business. In any EU/UK/US jurisdiction, you're paying 20–45% on that. In UAE, you keep 100%.

2. **0% corporate tax** (in free zones) — combined with no personal tax, your total tax burden is effectively $0 for years.

3. **MENA credibility is highest** for Qiyadon's regional focus — Dubai entity signals "we belong here."

4. **No VAT complexity until you're doing serious revenue** — 5% UAE VAT (if registered) is far simpler than EU VAT compliance.

5. **Global operational flexibility** — UAE entity can contract anywhere, invoice in USD/EUR/GBP, and you can open accounts in the UK (Wise), EU, or US.

**The honest tradeoff:** Annual cost is higher than UK/Delaware (~$5.5–16K vs ~$1–4K). But the **personal tax saving alone** — on a founder paying themselves £60K–£100K/yr — is worth £15–40K/yr in avoided personal tax. Net-net, Dubai is cheaper by far.

---

### 🥈 Backup: **England & Wales (UK LTD)**

**Use if:**
- EU/UK client contracts are being blocked because of a non-EU entity
- You need to be in an EU-adjacent jurisdiction and the UAE friction is too much
- Your revenue is under £90K (below VAT threshold, so minimal compliance)

**Why:** £700–1,800/yr annual cost is very competitive. 19–25% corp tax is acceptable. UK banking is seamless. EU/UK client credibility is gold standard. The downside is personal tax and no UAE-style zero personal tax.

---

### 🥉 Avoid for Now: **Germany**

Germany makes sense only if:
- You have a significant EU office with employees
- You need to hire German engineers/executives and need the credibility
- You're doing >€500K revenue where the 30% tax is acceptable in exchange for EU anchoring

**Not for bootstrapped, globally-mobile Qiyadon at this stage.**

---

### Avoid for Now: **Delaware**

Delaware makes sense for:
- US-incorporated businesses targeting US market
- Funded startups expecting VC investment (Delaware is required by US VCs)
- Businesses with US operations and US employees

**Not for Qiyadon** unless Ahmad is a non-US person with no US tax obligations, in which case Delaware is still not ideal for EU/MENA client focus.

> - $0 corporate tax
> - $0 personal income tax
