## 2. LLC vs C-CORP: WHICH IS RIGHT FOR QIYADON?

### Quick Comparison

| Factor | Delaware LLC | Delaware C-Corp |
|---|---|---|
| **Tax treatment** | Pass-through (no corporate tax) | Corporate tax + dividend tax (double taxation) |
| **Annual franchise tax** | Flat $300 | $175 minimum (scales with shares/assets) |
| **Annual report required?** | No | Yes — due March 1 |
| **Investor-friendly?** | Low — VCs avoid | High — VCs strongly prefer |
| **Can issue preferred stock?** | No | Yes |
| **Stock options for employees?** | No | Yes |
| **IPO-ready?** | No | Yes |
| **Complexity** | Low | Medium-High |
| **Best for** | Sole proprietorship, service businesses, passive investment | Startups seeking VC, scaling businesses |

### Recommendation for Qiyadon

**Start as a Delaware C-Corporation.**

Rationale:
1. Qiyadon is a **pipeline execution services business targeting international clients** — a C-Corp signals legitimacy to overseas customers and partners.
2. If there's **any possibility of VC or institutional investment** in the future, a C-Corp is effectively required. VCs cannot invest in LLCs in the standard preferred-stock structure they use.
3. VCs will often require conversion from LLC to C-Corp before writing a check — this costs legal fees and takes time. Starting as a C-Corp avoids that friction.
4. The annual franchise tax minimum for a C-Corp ($175) is actually **lower** than the LLC ($300) if you use the authorized shares method with ≤5,000 shares.
5. The "pass-through tax advantage" of an LLC is less relevant for a services business where profits are reinvested, not distributed.

**If Qiyadon will NEVER seek external investment and all profits flow directly to Ahmad as distributions → LLC is simpler.
Given the stated intent to potentially seek investment → C-Corp from day one.**

## 3. FULL REGISTRATION PROCESS (C-CORP)

### Step-by-Step

**Step 1: Choose a Company Name**
- Must be unique in Delaware
- Search: [Delaware Division of Corporations Name Availability Search](https://delaware.gov)
- Must end with: Corporation, Corp., Incorporated, Inc., Company, or Co.
- Example: "Qiyadon Corp" or "Qiyadon Inc."

**Step 2: Appoint a Delaware Registered Agent** *(simultaneous with Step 1)*
- Required by law. The agent has a physical Delaware address and receives legal/state mail on your behalf.
- You cannot use a P.O. box or your own Pakistan address.
- Cost: $50–$300/year (see Costs section)
- Recommended providers: Northwest Registered Agent, Harvard Business Services, Delaware Inc.

**Step 3: File the Certificate of Incorporation with Delaware**
- File with the Delaware Division of Corporations
- State filing fee: **$110** (this is the same fee whether LLC or C-Corp at the Articles stage)
- Processing time: 24 hours standard, $50 for 24-hour expedited, $100 for same-day
- You can file online at delaware.gov or through a formation service

**Step 4: Obtain an EIN (Employer Identification Number) from the IRS**
- This is your company's federal tax ID (like an SSN for a person)
- Required to open a US bank account
- **Non-residents WITHOUT an SSN:** Cannot apply online. Must apply by:
  - **Phone:** Call IRS at +1 267-941-1099 (Mon–Fri, 6am–11pm ET). Have Form SS-4 ready. EIN issued immediately on the call.
  - **Fax:** Fax Form SS-4 to 304-707-9471. EIN returned within ~4 business days.
  - **Mail:** Send to: IRS, Attn: EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999. Takes 4–5 weeks.
- Form SS-4 requires: company legal name, Delaware address, responsible party name/address/ITIN or passport info, description of business

**Step 5: Draft Corporate Bylaws and Appoint Directors/Officers**
- Bylaws are internal rules (can be done by founders without a lawyer for <$200 using template services)
- You need at least **1 Director** (Ahmad can be the director)
- Common officer roles: President, Secretary, Treasurer (Ahmad can hold all three)
- First board meeting: adopt bylaws, issue founder shares

**Step 6: File Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) Report with FinCEN**
- Required under the Corporate Transparency Act (CTA)
- File electronically at FinCEN's BOI E-Filing system
- Deadline: Within 30 days of formation (reduced from 90 days for 2025+)
- Report: Full name, date of birth, residential address, ID document for each beneficial owner (anyone with ≥25% ownership or substantial control)
- **Ahmad is the sole beneficial owner — he needs to provide his Pakistan address and passport number**

**Step 7: Issue Founder Shares**
- Document the issuance of shares to founders/members
- For a single-founder C-Corp: 100% of shares issued to Ahmad
- This is recorded in the corporate records (stock ledger)

**Step 8: Open a US Business Bank Account** *(see Section 6 below)*

**Step 9: Apply for a Delaware Business License**
- Delaware requires a state business license for most businesses
- Cost: **$75 per year** for a C-Corp
- Can be filed through Delaware's business licensing portal

## 6. BANKING

### Can a Non-US Resident Open a US Business Bank Account?

**Yes**, but it is challenging. Here are the realistic options:

### Best Options for Non-Residents

**1. Mercury** *(Recommended for tech/service businesses)*
- Fully online application
- Non-US residents accepted (including Pakistan)
- Requires: EIN, Certificate of Incorporation, Articles of Incorporation, Operating Agreement, passport
- **Requires a US business address** — the registered agent address can work, but some banks reject this
- FDIC insured up to $5M through partner banks
- Free wires, no monthly fees
- **Known catch:** Mercury has become stricter with compliance. Some Pakistan residents have reported denials. Apply early.

**2. Relay**
- Online, non-US resident friendly
- Requires a US business address
- Up to 20 sub-accounts (useful for managing client funds)
- Similar document requirements to Mercury

**3. Lili**
- Fully online, very non-US-resident friendly
- Does **not** require a US physical address in some cases
- APY on savings (up to 4%)
- Good for freelancers and service businesses
- May be easier to get approved than Mercury for Pakistan residents

**4. Wise Business** *(Not a US bank — a multi-currency account)*
- Excellent for international transactions
- USD account with US routing/account numbers
- Easy to open from Pakistan
- **Not a US bank account** — cannot receive ACH transfers from US companies easily, but excellent for receiving wire transfers and making international payments
- Best used alongside (not instead of) a US bank account

**5. Payoneer** *(Not a US bank)*
- Global payment platform
- USD account with US bank details
- Can receive US payments (ACH and wire)
- Popular with freelancers and B2B service providers internationally
- **Best as a supplement** for receiving client payments

### Traditional Banks (Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo)
- Require **in-person visit** to a US branch
- Very difficult for Pakistan residents
- Not recommended unless Ahmad has a US trip planned

### Documents Required for Any US Business Account

1. Certificate of Incorporation (Delaware)
2. EIN confirmation letter (IRS Form CP575 or 147c)
3. Operating Agreement / Corporate Bylaws
4. Board resolution or consent to open account
5. Valid passport (Ahmad)
6. Proof of Pakistan address (utility bill or bank statement, <3 months old)
7. US business address (registered agent address usually accepted)

### Recommendation for Qiyadon

**Open Mercury as primary** (for USD wires, checks, ACH) **+ Wise Business as secondary** (for international client receipts and currency conversion). This combination covers most use cases for an international services business.

## 8. PAKISTAN TAX IMPLICATIONS

### Does Ahmad Pay Tax in Pakistan on Delaware Company Income?

**Yes — as a Pakistan tax resident, Ahmad is taxed on his worldwide income.**

Pakistan's Income Tax Ordinance, 2001 imposes tax on the **global income** of residents. "Resident" is defined as someone who spends 182+ days per year in Pakistan, or whose Pakistan residence is the "usual place of abode."

**Key points:**
- If Ahmad lives in Pakistan (which he does), he is a Pakistan tax resident
- Any income he **rece**ives from the Delaware company — whether as salary, dividends, or distributions — is taxable in Pakistan
- Pakistan's corporate tax rate is **29%** (for companies) and **up to 35%** (for individuals in highest bracket)
- The US federal corporate tax is **21%** for the C-Corp

**This creates a potential double-taxation situation**, which the treaty (see below) partially addresses.

### Is There a Pakistan-US Tax Treaty?

**Yes — in force since 1959.**

Key provisions relevant to Ahmad/Qiyadon:
- **Foreign tax credit:** Ahmad can claim a credit in Pakistan for US taxes paid on US-source income, reducing double taxation
- **Reduced withholding:** US withholding tax on dividends, interest, and royalties from the US to Pakistan is capped at **10%** under the treaty (vs. standard 30%)
- **Business profits:** Covered under the treaty's "permanent establishment" article. If Qiyadon's management and control is exercised from Pakistan, the profits may be taxable in Pakistan regardless of where the company is incorporated

**Important caveat:** The treaty is old (1959) and limited in scope. It does not fully resolve the modern pass-through taxation of LLCs or the specific treatment of services income. Tax counsel in both jurisdictions is strongly recommended.

### Any Pakistan Reporting Requirements?

**Yes — significant ones:**

1. **Annual Income Tax Return (Pakistan):** Ahmad must file annually with the FBR and declare his worldwide income, including any salary, dividends, or distributions received from the Delaware company.

2. **NTN (National Tax Number):** Ahmad needs an NTN if he doesn't have one already.

3. **Beneficial Ownership Disclosure:** As a shareholder/director of a foreign company, Ahmad may need to disclose his ownership to the FBR under anti-money laundering regulations.

4. **Foreign Exchange Regulations:** Bringing money from the Delaware company into Pakistan (as dividends, salary, or loan repayment) must go through proper banking channels. The State Bank of Pakistan regulates inward remittances.

5. **Transfer Pricing:** If Qiyadon contracts with related parties (e.g., Ahmad's personal entity in Pakistan), transfer pricing rules apply. Keep transactions at arm's length.

6. **Sales Tax / GST:** If Qiyadon provides services to Pakistan-based clients, Pakistan's sales tax may apply. Services exported from Pakistan are zero-rated, but this requires documentation.

### Practical Advice on Pakistan Tax

- **Keep clean records** of all money flowing between the Delaware company and Ahmad personally
- **Use a Pakistani tax advisor** who understands cross-border corporate structures — this is not an area for DIY
- **Document the arm's-length nature** of any inter-company arrangements
- The **Pakistan-US treaty** provides some protection but is not comprehensive. Budget for tax advice in both jurisdictions
