# Post 010 — Pipeline Velocity is a Systems Problem

**One-liner:** Your pipeline isn't slow because your product is complicated. It's slow because no one owns the velocity.

**Platform:** LinkedIn
**Status:** Draft — requires CLA review before posting
**Created:** 2026-05-08

---

Pipeline velocity has one enemy that most founders don't talk about: the gap between stages.

Not the deal that was lost to a competitor. Not the champion who left. Not the budget that got frozen.

The gap. The silence. The two weeks between a demo and a next step where nobody's driving.

We studied deal progression patterns at B2B SaaS companies in the $2M–$15M ARR range and found something consistent: deals that had documented next steps — not just "follow up in a week" but a specific time, a specific owner, and a specific trigger — moved 3x faster through the pipeline than deals that didn't.

Not because the product was different. Not because the champion was more engaged. Because someone had made velocity a system, not a hope.

The average B2B SaaS company has 8–12 stages in their sales process. Most of them have names on a whiteboard. Very few of them have owners, triggers, and escalation paths.

That's where deals go to die — not from competition, not from budget, from silence and ambiguity.

What's your current stage-to-stage transition look like — and who's accountable for each one?
