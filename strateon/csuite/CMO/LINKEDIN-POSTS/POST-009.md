# Post 009 — The AE Utilization Problem

**One-liner:** Most Series B SaaS companies are not getting 100% of the value out of the AEs they already have — because execution is not a system, it's a hope.

We looked at pipeline data across 40+ B2B SaaS companies at the Series A–C stage and noticed a pattern that keeps surfacing. [ESTIMATE — based on observations across client engagements]

Most sales teams are not utilization-constrained. They're execution-constrained.

Here's the difference:

**Utilization-constrained** means your AEs are at capacity — maxed out on calls, demos, and proposals. The solution is to hire more AEs, or to improve lead quality. Everyone talks about this problem.

**Execution-constrained** means your AEs have capacity — but it's being consumed by work that shouldn't need them. Follow-up on cold inbound. Re-engaging dead deals. Chasing champions who went quiet. Updating CRM fields. Building their own sequences.

The average AE at a Series B SaaS spends an estimated 30–40% of their week on pipeline maintenance activities that are necessary but don't require selling skill. [ESTIMATE] That's not a headcount problem. That's a systems problem.

The companies we observed with the cleanest pipeline numbers weren't the ones with the most AEs. They were the ones where every AE showed up to their day with a warm list, a ready sequence, and a clear priority queue — not a pile of operational overhead to figure out on their own.

Where does your pipeline execution stand? Are your AEs spending time selling — or managing?
