Your marketing team ran a campaign. 50 leads came in.

Your sales team touched 8.

Nobody's talking about the other 42.

This is the handoff gap — and it's one of the most expensive silent leaks in B2B pipeline management.

**What the handoff gap actually is:**

Marketing measures MQLs. Sales measures closes. In between, there's a gap where leads go quiet not because they lost interest, but because nobody owns the middle.

The MQL gets generated. An email goes out. No reply. The record sits in the CRM with a "no response" tag. Six weeks later, someone runs a pipeline report and wonders why so many leads disappeared between stage 2 and stage 3.

The answer isn't that leads lost intent. The answer is that the system between marketing and sales has a structural gap — and it's not a people problem, it's a process problem.

**What we see in the data:**

According to research across SMB pipelines, the majority of leads that go dark do so within the first 72 hours after initial contact. Not because the lead was bad. Because the follow-up cadence never started.

Marketing handed off. Sales was busy. The lead sat.

**Why "improve communication" is not the fix:**

Telling marketing and sales to "communicate better" is a cultural fix for a structural problem. The real question is: who's running execution for the leads that fall between departments?

Not the CRM. It tracks.
Not the sales rep. They're focused on their own active deals.
Not the marketing team. Their job ended at the handoff.
