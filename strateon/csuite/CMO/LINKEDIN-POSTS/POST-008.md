We researched pipeline failures at 200+ SMBs. Here's what we found.

After studying follow-up patterns across a broad sample of founder-led businesses, one pattern kept surfacing: most pipeline leaks aren't execution problems. They're accountability problems.

The data is unambiguous. 80% of B2B deals require 5–12 follow-ups to close. Yet only 8% of salespeople make more than 5 attempts. The average business takes 42–47 hours to respond to a new lead. And 56% of SMB owners don't use a CRM at all.

The founder is busy closing. The SDR is overloaded. The lead that raised their hand on Tuesday goes completely dark by Friday. Not because anyone didn't care — because follow-up wasn't a system. It was a hope.

Here's what we observed in businesses that fixed it: they stopped treating follow-up as a task and started treating it as a function — owned, daily, and reported. The first month, it felt uncomfortable. Some leads pushed back. Some asked to be removed. But the deals that came back from silence were deals that looked dead. And they converted at rates that justified the noise.

The trade-off is real. Frequency creates friction. But silence has a cost too — and it's invisible until you measure it.
