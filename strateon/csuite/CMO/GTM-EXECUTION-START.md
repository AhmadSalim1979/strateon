### LinkedIn Post #1 — The Pipeline Problem Is Not What You Think

> Most B2B founders I talk to at the $1M–$3M mark have the same story:
>
> "We have leads. We're growing. But the revenue is lumpy — some months great, others quiet, and I can't quite figure out why."
>
> They assume it's a lead generation problem.
>
> It's almost never a lead generation problem.
>
> It's almost always a *pipeline mechanics* problem — the gap between "someone filled out a form" and "someone signed a contract" has leaks you're not systematically tracking.
>
> The firms that have solved this aren't doing anything magical. They've just made the implicit explicit: what's actually happening between your first touch and your close? Where do deals go quiet? What does your follow-up cadence actually look like vs. what you think it looks like?
>
> If you're a founder at an MSP, SaaS, or professional services firm and you've had a "quiet month" more than twice in the last year — that's not a sign you need more leads. That's a sign your pipeline has a leak.
>
> What's one leak you've noticed in your own pipeline? I've been mapping these for a while — genuinely curious what's showing up for others at this stage.

**[No CTA — post ends with a question. This is intentional. It starts a conversation.]**

---

### LinkedIn Post #2 — The $50K CRM Problem

> Here's a conversation I have at least once a week:
>
> Founder: "We need to get serious about our pipeline."  
> Consultant/vendor: "You need a CRM."  
> Founder: "How much?"  
> Consultant/vendor: "Well, enterprise is $30K setup, plus $2K/month, but there's a smaller plan for $800/month."  
> Founder: "...we have 8 people."
>
> The problem isn't that CRMs are bad.  
> The problem is that most small B2B firms are being sold infrastructure for where they're going, not for where they are.
>
> At $300/month, you don't need a $50K implementation. You need someone to look at the 8-step process you already have, figure out where deals are actually dying, and fix that specific thing.
>
> A founder with 5 employees and a well-tracked pipeline will outrun a 30-person company with a shiny CRM nobody updates.
>
> Has anyone else run into this — the "enterprise tool, startup team" mismatch? Genuinely curious how others have navigated it.

**[No CTA — ends with a question. Invites peer discussion.]**

---

### LinkedIn Post #3 — What I Learned Talking to 20 Founders About Pipeline

> I spent the last few weeks talking to founders — MSPs, SaaS shops, professional services firms — mostly in the $500K–$3M range, mostly founder-led.
>
> Here's what came up most:
>
> They're not struggling to *get* leads. They have leads.  
> They're struggling to convert them without it feeling like a second job.
>
> Specific patterns I heard:
> - "I follow up three times, then I feel like I'm annoying them, so I stop"  
> - "I don't know if the silence means they're not interested or just busy"  
> - "We hired someone to run our marketing and it was... fine. But not pipeline fine."  
>
> Every single one of these is a *systems* problem disguised as a motivation or effort problem.
>
> If you're a founder at this stage and this sounds familiar — I'd genuinely like to compare notes. What's your pipeline situation looking like right now?

**[No direct CTA. Soft invite to connect. Positioned as peer curiosity, not a sales meeting request.]**

## 3. Where Should the "Book a Free Pipeline Leak Audit" CTA Live?

**Use the main site — no separate landing page needed yet.**

Here's why:

The main site already has the new positioning and CTAs. The traffic we're generating in week 1 is *inbound from LinkedIn and Reddit* — these people are clicking through to Moosa's profile or the website. They already have context. A separate landing page adds friction and a second URL to track, with no meaningful conversion lift at this stage.

**What should change on the main site this week:**
- Ensure there's a **single clear CTA on the homepage** — "Book a Free Pipeline Leak Audit" with a button or link to a Calendly or Typeform. This should be above the fold or in the hero section.
- A dedicated `/audit` or `/contact` page that collects name, email, company, and brief context (2–3 fields max) is fine — but keep it simple.

**What to add to every LinkedIn post:**  
A soft call-to-action at the bottom: *"If this resonates — I've got a 30-minute slot each week where I just look at your pipeline situation and tell you honestly what I'd do. Link in comments."*  
(Link to the Calendly/Typeform on the main site.)

**When to build a separate landing page:**  
Only when you start running paid ads OR when you have enough inbound that tracking channel attribution becomes important. For now, the main site is sufficient.
