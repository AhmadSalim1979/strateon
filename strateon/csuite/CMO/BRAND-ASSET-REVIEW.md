## BRAND MARK CLARIFICATION — FOR BOARD ATTENTION

There is an outstanding question about the correct brand mark: **hexagonal "S" with internal lines** vs. **"S" with horizontal stripes (three horizontal gaps/cut-outs)**.

**Ruling:** Both treatments are approved — they serve different contexts.

| Mark Variant | Treatment | Approved Use |
|---|---|---|
| Stylized "S" | Three horizontal gaps/cut-outs | Wordmark integrations, covers, horizontal logo formats |
| Hexagonal "S" | "S" inside hexagon with internal geometric lines | Product UI, sidebar marks, app icons, profile images |

**Rationale:**
- The three-gap "S" was established first (Images 1–5) and remains the primary wordmark
- The hexagonal treatment (Image 8, Image 9) provides a contained icon form factor that works well in product UI and social profile contexts
- Both share the same "S" glyph — this is a container difference, not a brand inconsistency
- **Image 6 was correctly rejected** — it used four thin white *stripes* trailing from the left, which is a fundamentally different execution

**Board directive:** Design team may use either treatment based on context, provided the "S" glyph remains consistent (three horizontal gaps within the letterform). Do not introduce new "S" variants without a formal brand brief.
