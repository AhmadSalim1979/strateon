## Question 4: Scale Test Guidance (16×16, 32×32, 64×64)

The Board should test at these sizes with the following criteria in mind:

### 16×16 (Favicon — most demanding)
- **Critical test:** The teal S and navy hexagon must remain **legible as a single mark**, not a blur
- The angular S design is advantageous here — angular segments hold sharpness better than curves at tiny sizes
- Watch for: hexagon stroke becoming too noisy; teal S bleeding into white background
- **Pass threshold:** Recognizable as an "S in a hexagon" at a glance

### 32×32 (Favicon @2×, app icon small)
- Should be comfortably readable
- The bold stroke on the hexagon should have clear separation from the S
- This size is typically where the design "breathes" best for favicon use
- **Pass threshold:** Clear S letterform, visible hexagon outline, both colors distinct

### 64×64 (Larger favicon, app icon, small UI contexts)
- Should be sharp and clean with full detail intact
- At this size, the angular S design should look crisp and deliberate
- **Pass threshold:** All three S segments visible, hexagon corners sharp

### Universal Scale Check Criteria
1. **Color separation** — Navy vs. teal vs. white must remain distinct (no color bleeding)
2. **Stroke weight** — Hexagon outline must not disappear or become muddied
3. **S letterform integrity** — The three-part S must remain readable as a single character
4. **Geometric sharpness** — Corners should stay sharp, no anti-aliasing artifacts muddying the angular design
5. **Background contrast** — White background must remain clean, not gray or washed out

### Recommended test:
Render the asset at all three sizes on a white background. View at 100% zoom (no interpolation). The design should pass the "recognizable at a glance" test at 16×16. If any size fails, adjust the stroke weight of the hexagon outline — that is the most likely failure point at small scales.
