## Subject Line

The silent deal killer isn't your product

---

## Body

Here's something that keeps showing up in the data:

**48% of salespeople never make a second follow-up attempt after the first email gets no response.**

Not because they're lazy. Because it feels like the right call. Silence reads like rejection. Most sales training tells you to "move on to the next one." So you do.

But here's the problem with that logic:

80% of B2B deals require five or more follow-ups to close. The average conversion happens after the fifth touch. Yet most reps give up after one or two.

That gap — between what the data says works and what most teams actually do — is where deals go to die. Not because the product is wrong. Not because the price is too high. Because the follow-up stopped.

**The math is uncomfortable.**

If your team is making 100 first touches this month and abandoning 92 of them after four attempts or fewer, you're leaving the majority of your pipeline to rot. The leads are still there. The need is still there. The deal is still viable. You just stopped showing up.

This is what we call the follow-up gap — and it's more expensive than most founders realize.

**The firms that consistently close more pipeline have one thing in common:**

They treat follow-up as a system, not as a hope. They have someone whose job is to notice silence and act on it. They escalate when deals go dark. They have a forcing function that says "this deal hasn't moved in 12 days — do something."

That doesn't require a full-time head of sales. It requires execution discipline.

That's what we built Qiyadon to provide — pipeline execution that follows up, escalates, and reports until every opportunity has a clear outcome.

How many deals died in your pipeline last month simply because the follow-up stopped?
