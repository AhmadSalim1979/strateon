## 4. Pain Points Rising

**AI agent governance is the primary bottleneck — and most organizations don't have a framework yet.**
The May 1, 2026 data point — most organizations stuck in pilot phases due to governance challenges, not technology readiness — confirms that the AI agent adoption bottleneck is organizational, not technical. The pain point: deploying an AI agent that can act autonomously on business events requires an accountability framework (who is responsible when the AI agent makes a wrong decision?), an oversight structure (how do you monitor what the AI agent is doing?), and a security model (how do you prevent prompt injection, tool misuse, or memory poisoning?). Most organizations have none of these in place.

**AI agent security incidents are rising faster than the security infrastructure to prevent them.**
Palo Alto Networks, Portlock, Prisma AIRS 3.0, and documented attack patterns (prompt injection, tool misuse, memory poisoning, non-human identity compromise, supply chain attacks on agent frameworks) confirm that AI agent security incidents are rising. The pain point: the AI agent attack surface is growing faster than the defensive tooling to protect it. Enterprises deploying AI agents are ahead of their security teams in terms of understanding the threat model. The gap between AI agent deployment and AI agent security is the primary operational risk for enterprises in 2026.

**The EU AI Act August 2 deadline is 90 days away and trilogue negotiations haven't concluded.**
The May 13 trilogue date is the next milestone in the Digital Omnibus negotiations. If no agreement is reached before August 2, the original deadline applies. The pain point: organizations that have been deferring EU AI Act compliance preparation on the assumption of a delay are taking on significant planning risk. The cost of under-preparation (market access suspension for AI systems in the EU) is potentially larger than the cost of over-preparation (extra compliance spend).

**OpenAI's multicloud move creates fragmentation risk for enterprise AI deployments.**
OpenAI's move to AWS and Google Cloud means enterprise IT teams now have to manage AI agent deployments across multiple cloud platforms, each with different integration requirements, pricing models, and security assumptions. The pain point: multicloud AI is more operationally complex than single-cloud AI. The governance, security, and operational overhead for multicloud AI agent deployments is higher than most enterprises anticipated when they started their AI agent pilots.

**Claude Mythos autonomous exploit capability creates a new category of AI risk.**
Claude Mythos (autonomous security vulnerability exploitation) is a capability that has clear offensive applications. The pain point: defensive AI security tools (like Claude Security) are reactive — they defend against attacks that have been identified. Claude Mythos represents a class of AI capability that is inherently offensive and can be used to identify and exploit vulnerabilities before they're patched. The governance question for offensive AI capabilities is unresolved: who has authority to deploy AI systems that can autonomously exploit vulnerabilities?
