## 2. What Is Changing in the World

### Market Shifts

**Search behavior is structurally shifting — Google has a primary threat that isn't a competitor, it's a paradigm change.**
69% of AI assistant users now use Google less. 32% go to AI first for information, vs. 28% who go to Google first. This is the most important market signal of the quarter. Google's advertising model is built on search. If primary research behavior migrates to AI assistants, the search advertising revenue pool shrinks. This isn't immediate — Google still has billions of daily users — but it's the first time in 25 years there's a credible structural alternative to search-as-information-portal. The secondary effect: every brand's SEO strategy is now obsolete in planning horizon, not yet in practice.

**Enterprise AI revenue has crossed into real scale: AWS at $15B annual AI run rate, OpenAI enterprise at 40%+ of revenue.**
AI demand has moved from experimental to operational. The companies buying AI at scale are moving from pilots to production deployments. This is good news for AI vendors and bad news for companies that underinvested in AI integration — the competitive gap is widening. India leads in AI deployment (40% significant/full adoption vs. 28% global average), suggesting emerging markets may leapfrog in AI adoption the same way they leapfrogged landline infrastructure.

**AI pricing is shifting from flat subscriptions to usage-based models — the margin structure of the AI industry is changing.**
This is a B2B story that will eventually hit consumer pricing. Usage-based AI pricing aligns consumption with value, but it also creates volatile revenue for AI vendors and potentially higher costs for heavy users. The companies that built subscription-based AI products are now restructuring to capture usage-based revenue. This is a normal product cycle evolution, but it changes how enterprises budget for AI.

### Technology Shifts

**Agentic AI capability jumped from 20% to 77.3% real-world task success in 12 months — this changes what's deployable.**
Terminal-Bench data (April 2026) shows AI agents handling real-world tasks at 77.3% success rate, up from 20% in 2025. Cybersecurity agents at 93%. This is not a benchmark improvement — it's a deployment capability change. The question for enterprises is no longer "can AI agents do this reliably" but "which domains are ready and which still have the 'jagged frontier' problem." The jagged frontier (succeeding at cybersecurity but failing at telling time from an analog clock) is still real and still a deployment risk.

**Claude Mythos Preview + Project Glasswing = AI vulnerability discovery at scale, faster than human security teams.**
Anthropic's model found thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities that human review and automated testing missed for decades. This is a step change in the security research value chain. The implication: AI-powered offense and defense are both accelerating. Security teams need to assume AI can find vulnerabilities faster — the question is whether defenders can patch faster than AI-assisted attackers find. This tilts the advantage toward organizations with automated patch and deployment pipelines.

**NVIDIA Ising quantum AI models and Vera Rubin (H2 2026) confirm compute is the primary bottleneck.**
Ising open-source quantum AI models (April 14) for quantum processor calibration and error correction — up to 2.5x faster decoding, 3x higher accuracy. Vera Rubin (R100) focused on "Reasoning AI" coming in H2 2026. Data center power capacity at 29.6 GW. The competition for compute and energy is now a primary strategic constraint, not a scaling detail.

**The three-way frontier AI race is now clearly Google, OpenAI, Anthropic — xAI has organizational problems.**

## 6. AI Agent Capability Watch

### What's Improving Rapidly

**Agentic task execution in bounded domains** — Terminal-Bench 77.3% success (up from 20% in 2025) is a step change, not a marginal improvement. Cybersecurity agents at 93% are now meaningfully better than human security teams for specific vulnerability classes.

**Code generation and real-world software bug fixing** — Gemini 3.1 Pro at 80.6% on SWE-bench Verified. GPT-5.4 with 1M-token context and integrated Codex coding. Real-world code quality improving meaningfully.

**Multi-model routing efficiency** — The gap between "best model per task" theory and execution is narrowing. Function calling APIs and cost/latency modeling are improving.

**On-device AI capability** — iPhone 17's A19 chip with dedicated "Ultra Neural Engine" for on-device generative AI. Apple's hybrid Siri model (on-device + Google Gemini) demonstrates the practical architecture for on-device + cloud AI.

### What's Still Weak

**Reliable real-time information access** — All frontier models still struggle with current events, live data, and real-time web content. The hallucination-of-recent-events failure mode persists. Google Deep Research is the best workaround but is slow and resource-intensive.

**Unpredictable failure modes (jagged frontier)** — AI succeeds at PhD science but fails at analog clock reading. The capability is non-monotonic in ways that make deployment risk hard to price. Enterprises need domain-specific evaluation frameworks they mostly don't have.

**Cross-protocol agent interoperability** — A2A, OpenAI Agent SDK, Google Agent Skills still don't interoperate at protocol level. Multi-agent orchestration is still framework-locked.

**Full auditability for autonomous consequential decisions** — Agent systems can make decisions in regulated industries, but the explainability and audit trail for consequential decisions (financial services, healthcare, legal) still has significant gaps. The accountability frameworks for autonomous AI decision-making don't exist yet.

### Where Real Leverage Is

**Cybersecurity vulnerability discovery** — Claude Mythos Preview finding thousands of vulnerabilities missed by human review. AI-powered security research is now faster than human teams for vulnerability classes that require broad pattern matching across large codebases.

**Software engineering (bug fixing, test generation, code review)** — 80.6% SWE-bench success rate means AI coding assistants are genuinely productive at real-world software development tasks, not just toy problems.

**Research synthesis across many sources** — High value for tasks requiring synthesis, comparison, and summarization. Replacing some analyst workflows in specific domains.

### Where Hype Exceeds Reality

**Fully autonomous AI employees** — "Hire an AI agent to run a department" is not close. The 77.3% success rate is for bounded tasks. Ambiguous instructions, novel situations, and institutional knowledge remain human requirements.

**AI strategic decision-making** — AI can synthesize and recommend; it cannot replace human strategic judgment in genuine uncertainty with stakeholder complexity.

**AI creativity beyond remix** — AI generates excellent variations on existing creative patterns. It cannot genuinely innovate beyond its training distribution.
