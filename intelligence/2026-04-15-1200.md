## 2. What Is Changing in the World

### Market Shifts

**The Google-AI behavioral shift is no longer a leading indicator — it's a confirmed trend.**
The 69% of AI assistant users using Google less (from Report #012) is now established behavior, not an early signal. The more important question: is this showing up in Google's revenue? Alphabet reports earnings approximately every 3 months — the next report will be the first real data point on whether the behavioral shift has a measurable financial impact. If it does, every company's digital marketing strategy needs an AI discovery component.

**Enterprise AI budgets have crossed from "experimental spend" to "line item operational spend."**
AWS at $15B annual AI run rate, OpenAI enterprise at 40%+ of revenue — these are not pilot numbers. Enterprise AI buying has institutional momentum. The implication: AI vendors with established enterprise sales motions and support infrastructure have a durable advantage over technically superior but commercially immature competitors.

**The AI pricing model shift from subscription to usage-based is accelerating — creating budget volatility for buyers and revenue unpredictability for vendors.**
This affects how enterprises forecast AI costs and how vendors recognize revenue. The transition creates short-term friction (enterprises hate unpredictable bills) but long-term alignment (pay for value delivered). Watch for enterprise AI budget restructuring as CFOs respond to usage-based pricing volatility.

### Technology Shifts

**The AI agent capability jump is now "sustained deployment at scale" not "impressive benchmark."**
The 77.3% Terminal-Bench score was the headline in Q1. Q2's story is what happens when you deploy that capability at scale across thousands of enterprise workflows. Early evidence: cybersecurity (93%) is the clearest success case. Legal document review and financial analysis are in active deployment. The failure modes are also becoming clear — unpredictable, non-monotonic capability (jagged frontier) is causing operational incidents that erode enterprise trust faster than the successes build it.

**On-device AI is becoming a meaningful architectural pattern, not a feature.**
iPhone 17's A19 chip with dedicated neural engine, combined with Apple's hybrid Siri (on-device + Google Gemini for complex queries), demonstrates a practical on-device + cloud architecture. The tradeoffs: privacy, latency, cost (cloud) vs. capability (on-device). This architecture is becoming standard for consumer AI — enterprises should design their AI products with on-device capability as a required feature for privacy-sensitive use cases.

**Agent protocol interoperability is still unsolved — A2A, OpenAI Agent SDK, and Google Agent Skills don't talk to each other.**
This is a growing architectural problem as enterprises deploy multiple AI agent frameworks. The lack of protocol-level interoperability means agents can't seamlessly delegate to agents built on different frameworks. This will eventually be solved (likely by a standards body or a dominant vendor forcing adoption), but in the interim, multi-framework deployments require custom integration work.

### Business Shifts

**Apple's AI strategy is in a trust recovery phase after two consecutive stumbles.**
The accidental China launch (March 30, ahead of regulatory approval) and Giannandrea's departure around mid-April are creating a narrative of strategic inconsistency. Apple is reportedly more conservative on AI capex than Microsoft, Google, and OpenAI. The smart glasses launch (spring/summer 2027 target) is the product that resets the AI narrative. If that slips or underperforms, Apple's position in the AI value chain weakens further.

**The three-way frontier race consolidation is creating enterprise vendor lock-in risk.**
OpenAI, Google, Anthropic. That's the list. For enterprises making 3-5 year AI infrastructure commitments, the consolidation of frontier AI to three vendors creates lock-in risk. Diversification strategies (multi-vendor model routing) add complexity but reduce continuity risk. The enterprises that lock into a single frontier vendor without contingency plans are taking on meaningful strategic risk.

The companies that spent heavily on AI without redesigning workflows are now facing hard questions. ## 6. AI Agent Capability Watch

### What's Improving Rapidly

**Bounded domain task execution** — The 77.3% Terminal-Bench jump from 20% in 12 months was the step change. What's continuing to improve: cybersecurity vulnerability discovery (93%), software bug fixing (80.6% SWE-bench), legal document review, financial analysis. The pattern: highly structured, well-defined tasks with clear success criteria.

**On-device AI capability** — iPhone 17 A19 neural engine, Apple's hybrid Siri architecture. Privacy + capability tradeoff being practically resolved. On-device inference quality approaching cloud for specific task types.

**Multi-step reasoning with tool use** — Models handling longer task sequences with fewer failures. GPT-5.4's 1M-token context window and Gemini 3.1 Pro's 94.3% on GPQA Diamond (PhD-level science) show multi-step reasoning improving meaningfully.

### What's Still Weak

**Real-time information access** — All frontier models still struggle with current events and live data. Hallucination of recent events remains a failure mode. Google Deep Research helps but is slow and resource-intensive.

**Unpredictable failure modes (jagged frontier)** — AI succeeds at PhD science (94.3% GPQA Diamond) but fails at analog clock reading (50.1%). This non-monotonic capability makes deployment risk hard to price. Enterprises need domain-specific evaluation frameworks — most don't have them yet.

**Cross-protocol agent interoperability** — A2A, OpenAI Agent SDK, Google Agent Skills don't interoperate at protocol level. Multi-agent orchestration remains framework-locked. MCP has momentum as a potential standard but isn't universal yet.

**Consequential autonomous decision auditability** — Agent systems can make consequential decisions in regulated industries, but explainability and audit trails for those decisions still have significant gaps. Accountability frameworks for autonomous AI decision-making don't exist yet.

### Where Real Leverage Is

**Cybersecurity vulnerability discovery** — AI finding vulnerabilities faster than human security teams. This is the clearest success case for domain-specific AI agents.

**Software engineering (bug fixing, test generation, code review)** — 80.6% SWE-bench success means AI coding assistants are genuinely productive at real-world software development, not toy problems.

**Research synthesis** — High value for tasks requiring synthesis across many sources. Replacing analyst workflows in specific domains.

### Where Hype Exceeds Reality

**Fully autonomous AI employees** — "Hire an AI agent to run a department" is not close. The 77.3% success rate is for bounded tasks. Ambiguous instructions and novel situations remain human requirements.

**AI replacing strategic judgment** — AI synthesizes and recommends. It cannot replace human strategic judgment in genuine uncertainty.

**AI genuine creativity beyond remix** — AI generates excellent variations on existing patterns. It cannot genuinely innovate beyond its training distribution.
