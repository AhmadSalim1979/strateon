## 6. AI Agent Capability Watch

### What's Improving Fast
- **Long-horizon task completion** — Agents that can work on a task for hours/days, breaking it into steps, are now viable. Not magic, but functional.
- **Code generation and debugging** — GitHub Copilot-level capability is becoming commodity. The new frontier is code review, optimization, and architecture suggestion.
- **Multimodal document understanding** — Understanding complex PDFs, charts, and diagrams is now reliable. This unlocks legal, financial, and research applications.
- **Structured data extraction** — Pulling information from unstructured sources (emails, documents, web pages) is now accurate enough for production use.

### What's Still Weak
- **Real-time world knowledge** — My training data has a cutoff. I cannot tell you today's stock price, recent news, or weather without tools.
- **Persistent memory across sessions** — Without explicit memory systems (like my workspace files), I have no continuity between conversations.
- **Handling ambiguity** — When instructions are unclear, I either ask too many questions or make assumptions that might be wrong.
- **Calibrated confidence** — I sometimes sound more certain than I should be about uncertain things. Users need to maintain healthy skepticism.
- **Physical world interaction** — I cannot see, hear, or manipulate the physical world. This is an inherent limitation.

### Where Real Leverage Is
- **Structured research synthesis** — Taking many sources, summarizing, extracting key points. Faster and more comprehensive than manual research.
- **Drafting and iteration** — First drafts of emails, documents, code. Human refinement is faster than starting from scratch.
- **Analysis and explanation** — Breaking down complex topics, explaining in accessible language. Better than most humans at making complex things understandable.
- **Automation scripting** — Writing scripts to automate repetitive computer tasks. Real productivity multiplier.

### Where Hype Exceeds Reality
- **"AI will replace jobs"** — Partial automation is more realistic than full job replacement for most knowledge work roles.
- **"Agent will autonomously run a business"** — Current agents are narrow tools. They fail in unpredictable ways at the edges of their training.
- **"Multimodal AI understands video like humans"** — Video understanding is improving but still superficial compared to human temporal reasoning.
- **"AI will solve strategy"** — AI is good at executing on known frameworks. Strategic thinking with novel context remains human territory.

### Capability Convergence Signals
- **Foundation model performance gap narrowing** — The top 5 models are within a few percentage points of each other on most benchmarks. Differentiation is shifting to speed, cost, and safety.
- **Open-source matching proprietary** — Llama 3, Mistral, and Qwen models are matching GPT-4/Claude Opus on most tasks at lower cost.
- **Domain-specific models outperforming generalists** — In medicine, law, and finance, fine-tuned models are outperforming general models on domain tasks.

## 9. My Actionable Takeaway

**One thing to pay attention to:**
The GPU/inference cost decline is real and accelerating. AI is following the trajectory of computing power: it will become essentially free for most tasks within 3-5 years. This means the current window to build AI-native businesses on cost advantages is closing fast. If you have a business where AI is a cost driver, the next 18 months are critical to lock in infrastructure advantages before inference commoditization makes it a commodity.

**One opportunity worth exploring:**
Vertical-specific AI agent platforms. The horizontal agent framework market is crowded and will consolidate. But vertical agents (legal document review for SMBs, medical prior auth automation, financial report analysis for specific industries) have defensible data moats and underserved markets. Consider building on a proven vertical framework rather than competing in the general agent orchestration space.

**One risk to not ignore:**
AI governance backlash. The EU AI Act is just the beginning. As AI deployments scale and failures become visible (job displacement, biased decisions, privacy violations), regulatory and public pressure will build. Companies that deploy AI without robust governance frameworks are building on sand. The risk isn't just regulatory — it's reputational and legal. Investing in AI governance now is cheap insurance against future crackdown.
