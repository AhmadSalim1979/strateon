The experience we're proposing

Unhurried conversations, clear choices.

Proposed wording, for clinic review

A good dental visit starts before anyone picks up an instrument. It starts with being asked what brought you in, being listened to, and understanding what happens next.

The approach proposed for this website is simple: explain findings in everyday language, set out the realistic options and their costs, and leave room for questions before any decision is made. These are suggestions for the family and the clinical team to shape, not a description of how the practice works today.

i.

Listen before we look

A visit that begins with what you want to talk about, whether that's a specific worry or simply a check-up.

ii.

Explain before we treat

Findings, options, and what each involves and costs, discussed before treatment is agreed.

iii.

Care that lasts

An emphasis on prevention and sensible follow-up, so small problems are less likely to become big ones.

What happens when you enquire

From first contact to a clear plan.

A proposed journey for the practice to review, so patients know what to expect before they get in touch.

Proposed experience, for clinic review

  1. Getting in touch

    Once the clinic’s phone, WhatsApp or request channel is confirmed, you would contact the practice and say briefly what you’d like help with. Medical details would be discussed with the clinic directly, never through this website.

  2. Confirming a time

    The clinic would confirm a date and time with you. The proposal is that you are told who you will see, roughly how long to allow, and anything to bring.

  3. Arriving and settling in

    A short welcome at reception. How your medical history is collected and kept would follow the clinic’s own confirmed process. If you feel anxious, this is a good moment to say so.

  4. A conversation, then an examination

    The visit starts with what brought you in and what matters to you. The dentist then examines your teeth, gums and mouth, with X-rays only where they judge them clinically useful, and explains each step before it happens.

  5. Findings, options and your decision

    The dentist explains what they found and the realistic options, including doing nothing. You can ask what each involves and costs, ask for an estimate, and take time to think before agreeing to any treatment.
