Understanding Detention & Accessorial Charges

Detention

Detention refers to the time a driver spends waiting beyond the agreed free time at a shipper or receiver location. Free time is the period during which the carrier is not charged for the truck and trailer occupying dock space. Once free time expires, detention charges typically apply, compensating the carrier for the driver's and equipment's lost productivity.

Layover

Layover compensation is paid when a driver is held overnight or for an extended period awaiting a load. Unlike detention, which occurs at a single stop, layover typically involves a driver waiting for instructions or a load assignment at a terminal or other designated location. Layover fees are distinct from detention and are meant to cover the driver's time and expenses while waiting.

TONU (Truck Order Not Used)

TONU stands for Truck Order Not Used and is a fee charged when a scheduled pickup is canceled after the truck has already been dispatched to the location. This fee compensates the carrier for the time and resources expended in preparing for and traveling to the pickup, even though the load was not ultimately transported.

Lumper Fees & Other Accessorials

Lumper fees are charges for third-party dock labor hired to load or unload a truck. Other accessorial charges may include stop-off fees (for additional stops not originally agreed upon) and redelivery fees (when a shipment must be delivered to an alternate location). These charges are often overlooked but can add up to significant revenue for carriers.
