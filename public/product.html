Understanding Detention & Accessorial Charges

Detention

Detention refers to the time a truck spends waiting beyond the agreed free time at a shipper or receiver. This free time is typically defined in the load agreement and varies by broker or shipper. When detention occurs, carriers are entitled to charge a detention fee, though collection often proves difficult without proper documentation.

Layover

Layover compensation applies when a driver is held overnight or for an extended period awaiting a load. Unlike detention, which occurs at a single stop, layovers involve longer delays often due to scheduling issues or load availability. Carriers should track these instances separately to ensure proper compensation.

TONU (Truck Order Not Used)

TONU fees are charged when a scheduled pickup is canceled after the truck has already been dispatched. This fee compensates the carrier for the time and resources spent preparing for a load that ultimately did not materialize. TONU claims require clear documentation of the canceled appointment and the associated costs.

Lumper Fees & Other Accessorials

Lumper fees cover third-party dock labor costs for loading or unloading cargo, while other accessorials include stop-off charges, redelivery fees, and similar expenses. These charges are often overlooked but can add up significantly over time. Systematic tracking ensures carriers recover what they are owed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does ChargeGuard capture and document detention/accessorial time?

ChargeGuard uses carrier-provided data such as ELD/telematics feeds, geofence detection, or driver/dispatcher-submitted timestamps to capture arrival and departure times at each shipper or receiver stop. These timestamps are then applied to the specific free-time and accessorial terms that apply to that load, broker, or shipper to automatically calculate what is owed and generate a documented claim or invoice.

Can ChargeGuard work without an existing TMS?

Yes, ChargeGuard is designed to work with or without an existing TMS. It can ingest data directly from ELD/telematics systems, geofence detection, or manual entries submitted by drivers or dispatchers, making it accessible for small-to-midsize carriers who may not have a sophisticated TMS in place.

How does ChargeGuard ensure accuracy and evidence for claims that might be disputed?

ChargeGuard relies on timestamped data from trusted sources like ELD/telematics systems or manual entries to create a verifiable record of detention and accessorial events. This data is then applied to the specific terms of each load to generate a documented claim, providing a clear and defensible basis for any disputes.

How is ChargeGuard different from just tracking detention on paper or in a spreadsheet?

ChargeGuard automates the entire process of capturing, documenting, and calculating detention and accessorial charges. Unlike manual tracking methods, it uses real-time data to generate accurate, submission-ready claims, significantly reducing the risk of errors and omissions.
