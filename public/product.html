Understanding Detention & Accessorial Charges

Detention

Detention refers to the time a driver spends waiting at a shipper or receiver location beyond the agreed-upon free time. This free time is typically defined in the contract between the carrier and the broker or shipper. For example, if a load allows for 2 hours of free time and the driver waits 3 hours, the extra hour is considered detention time. Detention charges are meant to compensate the carrier for the driver's time and the truck's downtime, which could otherwise be used for another load.

Layover

A layover occurs when a driver is held overnight or for an extended period awaiting a load, distinct from detention at a single stop. Layover charges compensate the carrier for the driver's time and the truck's downtime during this extended wait. Unlike detention, which is typically charged by the hour, layover charges are often a flat fee for each day or partial day the driver is held. This distinction is important for accurate billing and recovery of accessorial charges.

TONU (Truck Order Not Used)

TONU, or Truck Order Not Used, is a fee charged when a scheduled pickup is cancelled after the truck has already been dispatched. This fee compensates the carrier for the time and resources spent preparing for and traveling to the pickup location. TONU charges are typically outlined in the carrier's contract with the broker or shipper and can vary based on the distance traveled and the time spent en route.

Lumper Fees and Other Accessorials

Lumper fees are charges for third-party dock labor used to load or unload a truck. These fees are often overlooked but can add up quickly, especially for carriers who frequently handle freight that requires additional labor. Other accessorial charges include stop-off fees (for additional stops not originally agreed upon) and redelivery fees (for failed delivery attempts). These charges are often owed but rarely tracked systematically, leading to significant revenue leaks for carriers.
