# Draft production policies: for clinic and legal review

**Status: DRAFT. Not legal advice. Not published.** These outlines are for the
clinic's own website. They must be completed and approved by the practice and
its legal adviser, with reference to applicable Pakistani law (including the
Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016, any data-protection legislation in
force at launch) and Sindh Healthcare Commission requirements. Qiyadon's
commercial terms are **not** dental-care policies and must not be reused.

## 1. Website privacy policy (outline)
1. Who we are: legal name of the practice, address, contact for privacy questions.
2. What we collect through the website: only what the chosen booking channel needs (name, phone, preferred time, optional short reason). No medical history online.
3. Why: to respond to appointment requests. Lawful basis or consent wording as advised.
4. Who can see it: named practice staff, plus processors (hosting, form or email provider) listed by name.
5. Where it is stored and for how long: retention period for unconverted requests; clinical records are governed separately.
6. Cookies and analytics: list them, or state that there are none.
7. Your choices: access, correction, deletion requests, and how to make them.
8. Changes and effective date.

## 2. Website terms of use (outline)
1. The website gives general information and is not a substitute for an examination.
2. An appointment request is not a confirmed appointment until the clinic confirms it.
3. Fees shown (if any) are indicative. A written estimate is given before treatment.
4. Intellectual property: the practice's name, logo and photographs.
5. Links to third-party sites.
6. Governing law and contact details.

## 3. Patient-information and medical notice (outline)
1. Health content is general, reviewed by the named clinician on the stated date.
2. Emergencies: localised Karachi guidance approved by the lead clinician.
3. Don't send symptoms, photos or medical records through the website or social media messages. Share them in person.
4. Consent: written consent for photographs, testimonials and case examples, and how to withdraw it.
5. Complaints: how to raise a concern with the practice, and the regulator's details (SHCC) as required.
